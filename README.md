# CLIA — Closed‑Loop Intelligence Architecture

![CLIA Logo Banner](https://copilot.microsoft.com/th/id/BCO.c48ddc99-ca04-4dfe-a376-423b1f100f37.png)

CLIA (Closed‑Loop Intelligence Architecture) is a tri‑domain intelligence framework designed by Hasan Kazmi.  
It integrates epistemic verification, probabilistic forecasting, and adaptive decision intelligence into a unified, closed‑loop system.

---

## 🔎 Overview

CLIA operates across three domains:

### 🧠 Epistemic Domain (Verification Layer)
- Serves as the truth firewall.
- Validates all inputs through source checks, base‑rate analysis, logic gap detection, and deception filtering.
- Confidence discipline enforced:
  - ≤75% ceiling for standard forecasts
  - 75–95% for near‑absolute verification
  - 95% maximum cap

**Output:** Validated intelligence signals.

---

### 📈 Analytical Domain (Forecasting Layer)
- Converts validated signals into structured forecasts.
- Detects global inflection points via signal mapping, pressure analysis, and timing precision.
- Forecasts remain probabilistic, never declarative.

**Output:** Verified forecasts and timing metrics.

---

### ⚙️ Operational Domain (Decision Layer)
- Translates forecasts into adaptive operational decisions.
- Classifies environments into regimes: Shock, Trend, Narrative, Compression, Transition.
- Each regime has defined action states: Ignore → Monitor → Enter Small → Scale → Max Exposure.

**Output:** Operational intelligence and adaptive response.

---

## 🔄 Unified Flow

**Verification → Forecasting → Action → Feedback**

CLIA guarantees that every forecast is evidence‑anchored, probabilistically disciplined, and regime‑aware.  
It forms a self‑correcting intelligence ecosystem that continuously validates, forecasts, and adapts.
